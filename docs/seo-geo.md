# Hanabi SEO and GEO guidelines

This is the working brief for the public site, the landing page, and the blog. It is written so design, engineering, and publishing can use the same language.

Hanabi is a product design and web development studio. The public site is [tryhanabi.com](https://tryhanabi.com). The studio builds simple, beautiful websites and digital products for founders and small teams: interface design, production front ends, brand systems, ecommerce, design systems, and small automations such as Telegram and Discord bots. The commercial action is an intro call at [cal.com/hanabi/30min](https://cal.com/hanabi/30min), or an email to workwithhanabi@gmail.com.

Search does not currently surface the domain. Treat this as a new entity. Rankings will come from a clear definition of the studio, pages that match how buyers search, and a blog that answers those searches with enough specificity to be cited.

## What the site is selling

People do not search for "your digital impression." They search for a partner who can make the company look credible and the product feel clear.

The offer, in the words the site should be willing to rank for:

- Product design and web development for founders.
- Marketing sites and landing pages that are fast, readable, and easy to launch.
- Brand identity and a small brand system, delivered so the team can keep using it.
- Frontend revamps and ecommerce rebuilds, including moves off older stacks such as WooCommerce.
- Product interfaces for early software: dashboards, MVPs, and focused web apps.
- AI automation and chat bots when a workflow is specific enough to deserve one.

Proof already on the site, and safe to mention because it is public work rather than an invented metric:

- Down the Cove: ecommerce restructure, migration, and redesign.
- Thomas Bewick: editorial site for a heritage craft brand.
- Ballarat Box Sports: community sports brand site.
- Wabisabi: calm brand site.
- Got Next and Leadly: product interfaces.
- Trade Moai: trading interface concept.
- A client portal for brand files, docs, and project updates after delivery.

The studio's own standard, and the line worth repeating because it is specific: clarity over complexity. Quality, clean code, a process that can change, speed, and plain communication.

## Keyword strategy

Head terms such as "web design agency" and "UI UX design" are owned by directories and firms with years of links. Chasing them produces pages that look busy and never rank. The useful queries are the ones a founder types while deciding.

Intent labels:

- **Buy.** They are comparing partners. These queries deserve a service section or a dedicated page, and a path to the intro call.
- **Do.** They are trying to solve the problem themselves. These queries deserve a blog post that teaches the decision, then offers Hanabi when the work should be handed to a studio.
- **Learn.** They are building vocabulary. These posts create topical authority and internal links. They should still end on a concrete next step.

### Blog keyword map

Each post in the editorial list at the bottom has one primary query. Supporting language should appear in the H2s and the excerpt, not as a repeated phrase in every paragraph. Keyword stuffing reduces both classic rankings and AI citations. The Princeton GEO study (KDD 2024, [arXiv:2311.09735](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735)) measured about a 10% drop in visibility for stuffing, against gains for citations, statistics, and quotations.

Write for the question behind the query:

- "how to redesign an ecommerce site" is a decision guide, not a services brochure.
- "core web vitals explained" is a plain-language engineering note a founder can forward.
- "how to hire a design studio" is a buying guide that is useful even if they never call Hanabi.

## Landing page guidelines

Use this when the homepage is redesigned. The visual standard stays Hanabi's. The information on the page has to survive a crawl and a skim.

### Title, description, and headings

- One H1. Keep it short enough to read on a phone.
- The title tag and the H1 do not have to be identical. The title tag should contain "product design" and "web development."
- The first 160 characters of body copy should say who it is for and what is made.
- H2s should be the real sections: services, selected work, why the process is different, who the studio is, how to start. Decorative labels can sit above them in small type, the way the page already does.
- Image alt text on work should name the project and the view, which the work data already does. Decorative marks can stay empty.

### Sections that earn the click and the citation

1. **Hero.** Brand line, one concrete sentence, intro call, link to work.
2. **Services.** Design, development, branding, plus the smaller offers (automation, bots, frontend revamp, design systems, ecommerce). Each block should say the outcome in one sentence.
3. **Selected work.** Project name, what it is, and a link. A later version gives each project its own page with the problem, the decisions, and the stack.
4. **How an engagement works.** A short sequence: intro call, scope, design, build, launch, portal handoff. AI answers quote sequences.
5. **Fit.** Who the studio is a good partner for, and which projects are better served elsewhere. That specificity is more quotable than a list of adjectives.
6. **FAQ.** Answer real buyer questions about timeline, required inputs, ownership, revisions, and delivery. Keep the answers useful even without a search enhancement.
7. **Final call.** The same intro-call action as the hero.

### Structured data

Add JSON-LD on the homepage, separate from the blog's article schema:

- `Organization` with name Hanabi, canonical url `https://www.tryhanabi.com`, logo, email, and the LinkedIn company URL `https://www.linkedin.com/company/hanabilabs`.
- `sameAs` for LinkedIn and Instagram (`https://www.instagram.com/okhanabi/`).

Article pages emit `BlogPosting` and breadcrumbs. [Google retired FAQ rich results in 2026](https://developers.google.com/search/updates), so FAQ schema is not a launch target. Every published post needs an accurate author, release date, hero image with alt text, an excerpt that can stand alone as the meta description, and sources when a claim depends on someone else's number.

### Technical base already in the product

- `app/robots.ts` allows all crawlers, including AI crawlers, and blocks `/admin`, `/portal`, `/cms`, and draft preview.
- `app/sitemap.ts` and `/blog/feed.xml` exist.
- Canonicals, Open Graph, and Twitter cards are set for the site and for articles.
- Blog media is specified at 16:9 and at 1200×630 for social.

Keep those. The landing page work is copy, structure, and schema, plus service and project URLs when the studio is ready to split the homepage.

### Performance and motion

The marketing site is already held to a high bar: static where it can be, reduced motion, and images from the marketing bucket. That is an SEO asset. Say it in plain language on the site. Google's Core Web Vitals targets are the ones to cite when a post needs numbers: LCP within 2.5 seconds, INP within 200 milliseconds, CLS under 0.1 ([Core Web Vitals](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)).

### Internal links

- Homepage sections link to the most relevant cluster, once posts are published.
- Every post links to one commercial URL: the intro call, the services anchor, the work anchor, or email.
- Posts link to one or two sibling posts in the same cluster.
- Project names in posts link to `/#works` until each project has a page.

## GEO: being cited, not just ranked

Generative engines quote passages. They do not "rank" a studio homepage the way a blue-link list does. The Princeton GEO paper is the research basis for the methods below. Apply them in posts and, where it stays readable, on the homepage FAQ.

| Method             | What to do on Hanabi                                                                                                                 |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Cite sources       | Name the publisher and link it. Prefer Google Search Central, Nielsen Norman Group, Baymard Institute, W3C WCAG, and the GEO paper.  |
| Add statistics     | Use a real figure from those sources, with the year and the publisher in the sentence. Do not invent studio metrics.                 |
| Add quotations     | Use a short attributed line from a public source, or a real client sentence already published on the site. Do not write fake quotes. |
| Authoritative tone | State the recommendation, then the condition under which it changes.                                                                 |
| Easy language      | Short paragraphs. Define a term the first time it appears.                                                                           |
| Precise terms      | Use the real names: Core Web Vitals, design tokens, lexical content, WooCommerce, Next.js.                                           |
| Varied vocabulary  | Do not repeat the primary keyword in every sentence.                                                                                 |
| Fluent structure   | Answer first, then the steps, then the limit of the advice.                                                                          |

Answer-first means the first two sentences can be lifted into an AI reply and still make sense. Example shape: "A seed-stage homepage has one job: make a stranger understand the product and see how to talk to you. The rest of the page supports that job."

### AI crawlers

`User-agent: *` with `Allow: /` already admits Googlebot, Bingbot, GPTBot, ChatGPT-User, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, and Applebot. Keep the portal and CMS disallowed. Do not block AI crawlers on the public site if the goal is citation.

### What makes a post citable

- A definition in the first paragraph.
- One numbered or bulleted decision list.
- One sourced number, only when it changes the advice.
- Three FAQs whose answers are self-contained.
- A named author (the Hanabi studio byline) and a date.
- A hero image whose alt text describes the picture, not the keyword.

## Blog editorial rules

Posts are drafted in Payload as unpublished drafts. Nothing in this program is published until it is reviewed.

### Shape of a post

- Title under 90 characters. Say the outcome or the decision.
- Excerpt of 80 to 220 characters. This is the search snippet. It should contain the point, not a tease.
- Meta title under 60 characters when it needs to be tighter than the H1.
- One H1, stored as the Payload title. The body starts at H2.
- **1,500 to 1,900 words** for the inbound library. That is long enough to finish the decision a founder is trying to make, and short enough that the piece does not start repeating itself. Posts under about 1,200 words lose to a more complete result on the same query. Posts past about 2,200 words on these topics usually pad. The first definition posts can sit a little shorter. The commercial and industry posts should hit this band.
- Three photographs: one hero and two inside the article. Each has its own alt text describing the picture. The images are calm, specific, and large enough to read. They are not collages of keywords.
- Three FAQs.
- Two sources from the approved list when the post uses outside facts. Omit sources rather than invent one.
- One hero image with specific alt text and a caption.
- One natural link to Hanabi. The link should match the post: a redesign post points at work or the intro call, a brand post points at branding, a hiring post points at the intro call.
- `noIndex` stays off. Draft status is what keeps it out of search until publish.
- Do not set a canonical override.

### Voice

Write like a careful studio, not a growth blog. Short sentences. Concrete nouns. The reader is a founder or a small team lead. Assume they are busy and slightly skeptical of agencies.

Avoid filler claims ("in today's digital landscape"), fake urgency, and invented numbers about Hanabi. It is fine to say "on a Hanabi project" when describing a way of working that the site actually states.

### Approved outside sources

Use these when a number or a definition needs a parent. Paraphrase. Do not paste long quotations.

- Google Search Central, helpful content: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content>
- Google Search Central, Core Web Vitals: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals>
- web.dev on LCP, INP, and CLS: <https://web.dev/articles/vitals>
- Nielsen Norman Group, how long users stay: <https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/>
- Baymard Institute, cart abandonment: <https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate>
- W3C WCAG overview: <https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/>
- Schema.org FAQPage: <https://schema.org/FAQPage>
- Princeton GEO paper: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735>
- Google Search Central, site names and titles: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/title-link>

### Images

Hero images are photographs with a clear subject: a desk, a workshop, a product, a store, a wireframe session. Alt text states what is in the frame. Captions may credit the photographer's platform. The image is not a keyword billboard.

Production delivery is Cloudflare R2 under the `blog/` prefix, using the Payload media collection. Drafts created before those credentials are available on the writing machine still need a media record, alt text, and the file kept so it can be uploaded to that prefix without renaming.

### Measurement

After publishing starts, review monthly:

- Queries in Google Search Console, especially the buy-intent list above.
- Clicks to the intro call and to email, not raw sessions.
- Which posts earn impressions for their primary query.
- Whether AI answers mention Hanabi for "design studio for startups" style questions. That will lag. The leading indicator is posts that are specific enough to quote.

Publish on a steady cadence rather than all at once. A new domain that releases a hundred posts in a day looks automated. The drafts can be prepared together. The publish schedule should be closer to two or three a week, clustered so each new post links to ones already live.

## The inbound library

The first 100 posts define the terms. The next 400 are written for a buyer who might hire a studio: a homepage, pricing page, proof page, or product surface for a specific kind of company. Each one keeps a single query, a concrete angle, and a path to Hanabi's services, selected work, or an intro call.

Length for those posts is 1,500 to 1,900 words, with at least six sections. Each post has three photographs: the hero and two placed after later sections. They stay unpublished until a person chooses a cadence of two or three a week. Releasing the library in one day would look automated and would make the posts compete with each other.

Assignments live in `content/blog-plan-500.json`. Slugs do not overlap the first 100.

## Publishing order

When drafts are approved, publish in this order so each cluster has a live hub before the spokes:

1. Studio practice: how to hire, how to read a portfolio, what the intro call decides.
2. Websites: first ten seconds, pages a seed-stage site needs, launch checklist.
3. One engineering pillar: Core Web Vitals in plain language.
4. One ecommerce pillar: what to keep in a redesign, tied to the kind of migration Hanabi has already done.
5. Then two or three posts a week, alternating a buy-intent post and a do-intent post.

The homepage title, buyer sentence, and Organization schema are the first search signals. Add a visible FAQ only when it answers questions real buyers have raised. The blog should send people to a page that clearly says what Hanabi is.
